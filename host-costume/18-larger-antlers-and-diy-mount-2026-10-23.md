## Headpiece build • recommended architecture, not yet a tested shopping kit

### Base and antler attachment

Use a **fitted, padded cap/helmet-style base with a rigid load-spreading area and rear adjustment**, hidden under the ivory hood. A thin fashion headband is not the proposed support for unknown-weight real antlers. No protective-equipment rating is claimed for an altered costume base.

For a **sound, clean joined skullcap**, keep the bridge intact where feasible, remove only the display plaque if the visible screws and actual underside permit it, and secure the bridge to the costume base at more than one point to resist rolling. Do not wear the wooden plaque. Existing display holes might help, but their suitability/condition is **not verified from the front photograph**. The Brooklyn display is a potential inspection example, not proof that its back unbolts cleanly.

For **separate sheds**, each burr/base needs its own rigid seat and anti-rotation restraint. No-drill strap/cradle mounting is worth testing **only if it positively traps the actual base**; loose ribbon, a single cable tie or glue alone is not the plan. Do not specify clamp dimensions until the base and mass are measured. If a cradle slips, a drilled mechanical mounting may be preferable; the host permits that trade-off. Exact hardware, antler-hole preparation and base materials remain unsourced/unselected, so this is not an instruction to drill an unknown resin casting or bone blindly.

[Celtic WebMerchant's antler-headgear build guide](https://www.celticwebmerchant.co.uk/blogs/tips-tricks-outfit-accessories/make-a-headgear-from-antler) is primary support for mechanical attachment to a broad sturdy base with padding, rather than adhesive-only mounting. Its real-antler method requires a drill and secure workholding; **it does not establish load capacity or comfort for our unchosen parts**. Work off the body; clamp the work and use suitable eye/dust protection. Do not cut or grind unknown old taxidermy materials as an improvised deadline shortcut.

### Veil and removal

The high gold veil needs a **separate lightweight support attached to the cap**, positioned between the antlers, with accessible release clips. Do not tension the fabric between sharp antler tips or make the veil bear antler weight. Keep the front clear for hosting/movement; lower only for photos/a short ceremonial cue. The actual canopy material, rail and fasteners remain build items, not sourced hardware in this pass. [17's textile leads](17-hollow-court-sourcing-2026-10-23.md#veil--closer-texture-separate-from-headpiece-readiness) remain research, not selected orders.

### Physical gate before a final buy/build/render

1. Choose by actual shape, **straight-line height, worn width, base footprint and pair mass**. Seller beam/tine lengths, whole display weight and photo perspective are not substitutes.
2. Inspect unaltered pieces for cracks, loose joins, brittle edges or retained tissue. Weigh antlers without display hardware. No published comfortable-mass threshold was established; do not invent one.
3. Mock up the mounting orientation before drilling or cutting. Test at the sink/table first, then seated on the head with face clear, followed by turns, bending and removal. Stop if it shifts, needs a hand to steady it or causes pressure.
4. Only after stability is established, add the independently removable veil and rehearse **30–45 minutes** with actual room lighting. Remove for crowded dancing if unstable or prone to catching. No all-night comfort or safe hugging assumed.
5. Build in hand by **October 29–30** to allow fitting. Before October 31 arrival alone is insufficient if a major construction job is still outstanding.
