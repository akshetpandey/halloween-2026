## Sound expectations and verification

[Audimute's FAQ](https://www.audimute.com/faqs) distinguishes absorption of reflected sound within a room from blocking sound transmission. [Rose Brand's acoustical guide](https://mcprod.rosebrand.com/products/fabric/acoustic-technical-fabrics/acoustic-fabrics-and-foam) explains that weight, fullness and mounting matter; its published acoustic ratings refer to specified pleated test assemblies. Do not transfer those ratings to our poplin, fleece or U-Haul pads. Ordinary thin wall cloth/pads do not establish useful isolation of low-frequency dance music.

Aim for a less sharp/echoing room at moderate playback level. Neither padding nor the low-pile rug promises a quiet neighboring apartment. If neighbor noise is the main objective, the music/operations plan must address actual level, bass and speaker coupling; no DSP or playback changes performed here.

Compare the same short spoken/music passage at the same speaker position and level before/after the trial; listen from several spots for harsh reflections and bass buildup. No percentage/dB estimate without measurements. Check actual pads for smell/shedding, support strain, window opacity, equipment clearance and party traffic. Keep only useful, secure treatment. Add about **0.5–1 hour hands-on** for the backing trial/attachment; physical tests remain unperformed.
