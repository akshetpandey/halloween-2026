## Applied edits

1. Require a specific relationship to an exact reference recording. Name the rhythmic connection and the hook, vocal or melodic connection. Adjectives such as happy, catchy, sexy and energetic cannot establish fit by themselves.
2. Keep the dance-led center as a working interpretation of the host's four references. Grove 17 finished **0/50 Keep**, including all seven club/garage comparisons. The votes do not prove that house succeeds, indie fails or softness caused a Cut.
3. Check musical fit, lyrical fit and pop risk independently. Earlier candidates carried known mixed lyric and pop-facing caveats despite the confirmed brief. For the next round, defer unclear/mixed lyric contexts and obvious pop-boundary tests. This is conservative sourcing for this round, not a change to earlier approvals.
4. Preserve dynamics. The main sections should maintain movement, with departures that return with purpose. Repetition can support a dance track when phrasing, percussion, bass, melody or changes in density sustain interest.
5. Keep the pop boundary honest. Approved chart-adjacent recordings do not justify a mainstream-artist ban, popularity cutoff or obscurity requirement. A candidate still needs a concrete relationship to an anchor and an independent pop-risk check.
6. Replace the proposed separate 12-track calibration with the newly authorized 50-track round. Use the first 12 positions to make the direction easy to assess, without another approval checkpoint.
7. Distinguish listening from inference. Metadata, provider descriptions and catalog recommendations cannot establish that the agent heard a bassline, vocal delivery or full arrangement. Record evidence and remaining uncertainty with the candidate.
