## Hook-and-clip approach
Use lightweight matte black cloth in separate panels, held along the top by fabric/binder clips hung from removable wall hooks on a suitable smooth finished surface. Adhesive bonds the hook to the wall; clips grip the fabric. No adhesive bond to fuzzy velvet/felt is required. Fold the top edge to give clips more purchase. Test clip slip and fabric tearing before hanging a full-height panel.

This is a proposed installation method, not a manufacturer's certified fabric-hanging system. Choose hooks for the actual surface and panel weight; follow each selected product's preparation, cure time, orientation and removal directions. Do not add several hook ratings together and assume the resulting number is a safe system load. Spread the top supports so fabric hangs vertically with little side tension. Do not stretch a cord between adhesive hooks; it changes load direction. Place panels outside shoulder/hand traffic so dancers do not pull them. Heavy lined velvet needs a more substantial support plan.

On smooth compatible painted walls this is worth a small prototype. On textured/peeling paint or wallpaper, choose a suitable mechanically fixed support, clamp to an existing suitable structure, or a compact freestanding support with a protected footprint. Do not promise damage-free removal from unknown paint. Ordinary adhesive-backed felt, carpet tape, duct tape, generic mounting tape and glued hook-and-loop are not the default wall covering: removal and paint damage are the main drawbacks.

[Command picture strips](https://www.command.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b5005604177/) specifically exclude fabric and textured/rough surfaces. That rules out sticking regular picture strips directly to the back of velvet. [Command application/removal guide](https://www.command.com/3M/en_US/command/how-to-use/) and [hook weight guidance](https://www.command.com/3M/en_US/command/how-to-use/product-weight-limits/) are mounting references; exact hook SKU and count wait for surface/load checks.

## Which fabric?
| Material | Fit for this room |
|---|---|
| Lightweight black duvetyne / matte stage cloth | Preferred first sample for a dark, low-reflection cave backdrop. A brushed surface gives more depth than shiny polyester; thin versions may transmit window light. |
| Lightweight black velour | Good when soft velvet texture is important. Check actual weight, opacity and sheen; avoid assuming upholstery velvet is suitable for removable hooks. |
| Ordinary black felt | Useful for small flat scenic pieces. For broad walls, seams and stiffness can look more like a noticeboard; adhesive-backed versions make removal harder. Not automatically blackout or acoustic treatment. |
| Existing shortlisted ready-made velvet curtains | Remain an alternative if the final weight/support works. Lack of rods does not itself require raw yardage; the upper edge can potentially be clipped, but verify construction and weight. |

For extensive fabric near party lights, prefer a fabric with documented flame-retardant performance. Keep cloth clear of heaters, lamps, vents, equipment cooling and exit/window hardware. This is a material preference, not a claim that a particular local code requires it. Fabric scenery is not soundproofing and will not solve transmitted bass.

## Coverage and appearance
Start with the DJ backdrop and bedroom windows rather than committing every wall. This preserves floor space and lets us test darkness/temperature. Plain black walls need only slight loose folds; roughly 1.2–1.5 times the visible width is a planning range, not a mandatory purchase multiplier. At windows use separate liftable/unclippable panels and test against actual outside light. Do not seal the only usable ventilation route or obstruct needed window access.

Raw fabric may need cutting to length and edge finishing. Host's premade-only requirement was for the NFC moon stickers, not a blanket ban on all fabric work; nevertheless confirm finishing workload before buying yardage. No sewing is assumed for the prototype: clips can hold a folded top, and the bottom can be folded/clipped temporarily while testing. Final fabric-specific hems/edge treatment follow selection. Keep bottoms clear of the floor and feet.

Black fabric absorbs much of the planned red/violet light: aim some light at the guardian/branches or a selected textured surface so the room still has visual depth. Don't rely on washing black cloth to provide all room visibility.
