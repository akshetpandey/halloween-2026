# Living room • The October Grove
Status: Concept ready; photos/measurements pending. Owner: build lead TBD. Target: prototype Sep 20, installation Oct 30.

## Guest experience
A warm forest clearing at night: drinks on the kitchen counter, four seats around a low table, a closer couch/TV nook, and a fairy-ring focal point. Preserve the main social area and sightlines between people.

## Layout before decoration
Locate the entrance, kitchen queue, bedroom doorway and bathroom route first. Put the scenic forest cluster in a dead corner or against a wall, never at a pinch point. Keep the couch close enough to the TV for intentional viewing but leave room to pass without crossing in front of seated guests. Four seats at the coffee table can be a mix of existing couch/chair/stools; confirm which arrangement physically fits.

Put cups at one end of the kitchen counter, labeled punch in the middle, and water at the other end or a separate surface. Guests should not have to backtrack through the drink queue to reach water.

## Build in descending value
1. **Moon wall:** one large lightweight paper/card moon, roughly 24–36 inches only if wall scale supports it. Light indirectly with a cool/neutral lamp. Use rated wall attachments suitable for the actual surface.
2. **Two tree silhouettes:** cardboard trunks or lightweight branches against a wall, supported independently. Layer sparse leaf shapes and amber LEDs.
3. **Contained forest floor:** two or three shallow trays with fabric/faux moss, small pumpkins and attached leaves. Keep moss and loose natural material off walking routes.
4. **Fairy circle:** a tabletop ring of 7–9 small mushroom props around the offering bowl, or a scenic ring inside an inaccessible corner tray. The ring is a visual motif rather than an obstacle people step over.
5. **Window forest:** removable paper branch silhouettes on selected panes or a freestanding fabric panel. Leave operable/egress windows accessible. Do an after-dark street-side check so the result reads as branches, not random clutter.
6. **Table:** dark runner, low moss tray and two LED candles; reserve most surface area for cups.
7. **Optional upper canopy:** only after the room works without it. Use very light leaf garlands on suitable rated supports, away from detectors, vents and lighting. Do not suspend real wood over guests using adhesive hooks.

## Materials allowance
Suggested scenic allocation $180 from the $1,800 plan; shared lighting, mounting hardware and drapes are budgeted elsewhere. Start with borrowed branches/planters and cardboard rather than buying artificial trees.

## Build steps
- [ ] Photograph bare room and measure the four functional zones.
- [ ] Mark scenery footprints using removable tape tested on the finish.
- [ ] Build one tray and one trunk section; photograph at night.
- [ ] Choose final quantities after the prototype.
- [ ] Attach leaves/moss to removable scenic bases.
- [ ] Mount scenery, then lighting, then tabletop detail.
- [ ] Run the television muted and check that its brightness does not wash out the moonlit feel.
- [ ] Walk all routes while carrying a drink; check from seated height as well.

## Completion test
Four intended seats work, the couch nook remains accessible, the drinks queue does not block a doorway, and every guest walking route is firm and clear. The room feels like a forest before adding a ceiling canopy.
