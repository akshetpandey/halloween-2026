## The story in one breath

On Halloween, a hidden woodland court borrows an apartment to hold its last gathering before winter. Its creatures remember every visitor they have ever entertained, yet one name is missing from tonight's preparations. They ask masked guests to earn their attention, bring new people into the gathering, and judge the apparitions around them. As the night deepens, the missing name begins to sound less like a forgotten ancestor and more like someone in the room. At closing, the Antler King reveals that his season has ended: the Court has been learning the name of its next sovereign.

## Private truth — host and helpers only

The Hollow is a refuge at the edge of the ordinary world. Each autumn it appears wherever people make a night worth remembering. Tonight its roots reach into Williamsburg: the living room has become October Grove, the bedroom its dancing Hollow, and the bathroom its Moon Well.

An old compact lets the wood keep a mortal name through winter. That person holds the office of Sovereign of the Hollow Court until the next turning. Their duty is fairy-tale hospitality: to stand for a place where strange people may be welcomed. It creates no actual obligation for the guest who receives the honor.

The host is the outgoing Antler King. As his season ends, his name is fading from the Court's winter memory. He remains himself, wearing his antlers, but can no longer supply the name for the coming season. The old law prevents him from appointing a successor. The wood must hear three kinds of testimony: its creatures' recognition, welcome extended to newcomers, and the assembled guests' judgment.

The creatures agree that a name is needed. They disagree extravagantly about what makes a person memorable. Each therefore offers its own small test. They know why the gathering is happening; their withholding is courtly mischief, not amnesia that a puzzle magically cures. A few are helpful, a few vain, one would rather get back to dancing.

The apparent mystery is **“Whose name has the Court forgotten?”** The answer concerns a person it is only now coming to know. The guests make the evidence through their own actions. At closing, the game's agreed scoring rules identify the name the story will honor.

The stakes are wistful rather than catastrophic: the wood wants someone to remember through the dark season. Nobody is trapped, cursed or responsible for saving the party. No universal completion target, collective energy meter or extra quest is introduced. Low turnout, unfinished collections and unused Summons remain ordinary game states.

## Four acts, experienced at each guest's pace

| Act | Guest experience | Story movement |
|---|---|---|
| I — Something has noticed you | Registration and first Favor | Ordinary decorations become inhabitants. A creature learns the guest's name. |
| II — An unfinished gathering | 3–6 Favors, including the first Summons | There is a blank in the Court's preparations. The creatures need more than clever answers: they need people and judgment. |
| III — The name is among us | 8–15 Favors, including the second Summons | Evidence points toward the living guests. Each small interaction begins to resemble testimony. |
| IV — The wood remembers | Event-wide finale after results are frozen | The Antler King explains the compact, reveals the succession rite and recognizes the new sovereign. |

The guest's practical objective stays simple: **find creatures and earn Favor**. “Find the missing name” is a dramatic question, never an extra answer field or a scavenger hunt for letters. There is no final riddle, mandatory room order or required conversation with the host.

Chapters use the existing proposed count thresholds. A three-Favor guest and a fifteen-Favor guest hear the same self-contained finale. Late arrivals get the opening and their own encounters; no live scene must be caught to understand the ending. Archive passages for rereading, with short dismissible presentation during play.

## Rooms as parts of the story

**October Grove — the place that remembers.** The golden tree keeps the records; the moon witnesses the gathering. Warmth beneath the dim purple light makes this feel like an invitation into somewhere slightly impossible. Its creatures favor conversation, memory and judgment. Guest line: “The roots have reached this house. They are learning who lives above them.”

**The Hollow — the place that remains alive.** The giant guardian carries the Court's pulse while the gathering lasts. Smaller creatures at the room's perimeter are as interested in audacity and absurdity as solemn ritual. Guest line: “The thing behind the music has been awake longer than the building.” No beat matching, audio clue, DJ interruption or access to equipment is required.

**Moon Well — the place that lets go.** Its witness understands that every ending must leave space for an arrival. Guest line: “The water remembers what the branches release.” The scored encounter is outside the bathroom. Entering the bathroom, using water or taking a gift is never necessary to solve it.

These are interpretations of the planned scenery. They require no additional installation or synchronized lighting cue.

## Fifteen creatures with different reasons to notice you

All names and associations in this section are **proposals** against the current slot IDs. Physical choices, especially DEC-04/05 and purchased animals, still need review. If a prop changes, adapt its character before production. Sigils and art remain future work; no puzzle solution may depend on these stories or an illustration.

### Five presences of the house

**DEC-01 · The Moon That Stayed — glowing moon.** An immense, courteous observer, quietly vain about its powers of attention. It admires people who look carefully. Greeting: “I have watched the whole evening. You may begin by noticing one thing.” Earned lore: “It was invited for a moment, several centuries ago. Nobody has found a polite way to tell it the evening is over.”

**DEC-02 · The Gilded Root — golden tree.** The Court's exacting archivist; offended by careless records, secretly delighted by new entries. Greeting: “Names fade. Impressions last. Let us see which you intend to leave.” Earned lore: “It writes the gatherings beneath its bark. This year's record contains a space it has refused to fill from memory.”

**DEC-03 · The Keeper of the Pulse — giant DJ guardian.** An ancient celebrant who believes a gathering should feel alive. Greeting: “Yes, I can hear you thinking. Very industrious. Show me.” Earned lore: “Long before this house had walls, it kept time for things with too many feet. It considers the present arrangement an improvement.”

**DEC-04 · The Listener Below — proposed Moon Well threshold.** Gentle, laconic, at ease with endings. Greeting: “You may leave a moment here. Take as long as you need.” Earned lore: “It remembers the sound a name makes when it is finally released. Tonight it is listening for the space that follows.”

**DEC-05 · The Unfinished Guest — proposed skull/branch shrine.** A skeletal remnant of a visitor who lingered for an excellent story. Its humor distinguishes the shrine from the separate raven figure. Greeting: “I meant to leave hours ago. That was several autumns back.” Earned lore: “It has witnessed many turnings. At every one, it announces that this will be its last. Then someone interesting arrives.”

### Five visitors from the wood

**BUY-01 · The Black Correspondent — raven candidate.** A gossip who treats every observation as a scoop. Greeting: “I have heard three versions of you already. Make this one worth repeating.” Earned lore: “It carries news between gatherings. When asked why tonight's record is unfinished, it says it never prints a name before the evidence arrives.”

**BUY-02 · The Very Certain Owl — barn owl candidate.** A fussy adjudicator who prizes reason and occasionally changes its mind in private. Greeting: “An opinion is a start. I prefer something I can defend.” Earned lore: “The owl once issued a judgment so immaculate that nobody understood it. Tonight it has been asked to keep things brief.”

**BUY-03 · The Fox of Small Print — fox candidate.** Delighted by cleverness, scrupulous about stating a bargain's actual terms. Greeting: “The terms are before you. I do recommend reading them.” Earned lore: “The fox has never broken the Court's law. It is unbearable about this. It maintains that noticing a rule is a kind of courtesy.”

**BUY-04 · The Stag at the Edge — stag candidate.** A watchful woodland guest, separate from the host's Antler King persona. Greeting: “There is room to approach. I have left it for you.” Earned lore: “It stands where paths meet, keeping one clear for whoever arrives last. It believes a gathering can be judged by the space it leaves.”

**BUY-05 · The Hare of Almost — hare/rabbit candidate.** Restless, encouraging, utterly unembarrassed by another attempt. Greeting: “Almost is a very useful place. I pass through it constantly.” Earned lore: “It has arrived late to every gathering and somehow heard every important thing. It insists that another attempt is different from starting over.”

### Five small things the gathering has made

**MAKE-01 · The First Knock — ochre-bound forked twig.** The spirit of someone making the first approach. Greeting: “Someone has to begin. It might as well be you.” Earned lore: “It grows wherever a stranger is offered a place. The fork in its head points both inward and out.”

**MAKE-02 · The Ember Guest — rust-sashed straw fan.** Warm, dramatic, determined to make an impression. Greeting: “Very well. Let us give the evening something to remember.” Earned lore: “It remembers no fire, only the people who once sat around one. Its straw is cold. Its accounts of those evenings are not.”

**MAKE-03 · The Knotkeeper — indigo crossed twigs.** Patient and exact, interested in relationships rather than hierarchy. Greeting: “Nothing here stands entirely alone. Attend to what joins it.” Earned lore: “It remembers a gathering by who introduced whom. Ask for a name and it will tell you a story with at least three people in it.”

**MAKE-04 · The Little Host — moss-collared straw bundle.** Bustling, generous, mildly offended by an unattended guest. Greeting: “Come closer. There is enough room if everyone behaves sensibly.” Earned lore: “It was made from what remained after a harvest. It has spent every autumn since insisting that there is enough to share.”

**MAKE-05 · The Open Door — ivory-veiled twig arch.** A quiet presence that cares about what comes next. Greeting: “An opening is useful. Try leaving one.” Earned lore: “Its veil moves whenever someone new enters the gathering. Even when the windows are shut.”

Together these voices make a Court with arguments and affection, rather than fifteen interchangeable quest dispensers. The household presences supply continuity; the animals supply opinion and comedy; the handmade figures embody the small acts that create a gathering. All fifteen award the same one Favor under the existing rules; these groups are not factions or scoring classes.

## Making the existing interactions feel like the story

**Puzzles are auditions for attention.** A creature sets a small demand because that is how it makes a judgment. The greeting supplies personality; the puzzle supplies complete, literal instructions on the screen. Lore remains optional and never supplies a hidden clue, password or required fact. Keep all fifteen families unassigned until the planned mechanics/roster review and playtests.

Generic encounter copy, usable before assignments are chosen:

> Success: “You have its attention. Its Favor is yours.”
>
> Retry: “The creature waits. Try again.”
>
> Already earned: “It remembers you. Its testimony stands.”

**The Bestiary is a record of acquaintance.** Earned cards hold an individual voice and a keepsake image, not fragments of a code that needs all fifteen pieces. No lore line instructs the player to open cupboards, move a figure, approach the DJ gear or visit a second entity first.

**The Looking Glass lets the creatures consult the guests.** Keep the existing question, “Which guise has captured the Court's attention?” The fiction concerns memorable apparitions; the real choice concerns the two displayed costumes. No extra personality vote or moral assessment is added. Votes remain optional and preserve already-earned Favor. At the finale, explain how this testimony contributes through the agreed costume award; it does not independently elect the sovereign.

**Standing is attention made visible.** Keep the existing navigation and podium. Optional framing: “Some names carry farther through the branches.” The top three are current leaders only; no pre-finale titles of office or implied final winner while the costume judgment remains pending.

### First Summons — a place for someone new

Unlock at the fourth distinct entity Favor, using the existing deferrable invitation flow.

> **MAKE ROOM BENEATH THE BOUGHS**
>
> The Court has heard your name. Carry this invitation to someone here whose name it has yet to learn.

Recipient copy:

> “Someone within the gathering has made room for you. The creatures are listening. Enter the Hollow Court.”

### Second Summons — keep the circle open

Unlock at the tenth distinct entity Favor.

> **LEAVE THE DOOR OPEN**
>
> You know your way among the creatures now. Find someone here who does not. Let this become their evening too.

Recipient copy:

> “The gathering has kept an opening. This invitation carries you through it. Enter the Hollow Court.”

Both invitations have the same mechanics. Plain helper text should explain that an unused Summons is for a previously unregistered person already at the party, who chooses to complete registration. Show the existing QR/link and **Not now**. Joining is voluntary; the fiction never instructs people to bring extra guests to the apartment, disclose another person's identity, or perform a pledge. Redemption copy: “A new name has entered the gathering.” Apply the game's agreed referral point separately. No automatic Favor for the newcomer.

## Rumors and the Antler King's behavior

Suggested casual lines for the existing volunteers, used when conversation allows:

- Early: “The creatures are taking attendance. Apparently the tree found a blank in its records.”
- Middle: “The owl thinks it's a clerical error. The raven is being very smug.”
- Later: “I've heard they're waiting for a name. I don't think it's an old one.”

These are optional alternatives to the [runbook's gossip](../volunteer-runbook.md), not scheduled scenes. A helper can demonstrate the ordinary phone flow immediately afterward. One rumor is enough; no guest needs to hear all three.

The host behaves like someone enjoying a final evening of responsibility, not a secret villain or a missing person. If asked what the Court wants: **“The wood keeps its own counsel. I am trying, with limited success, to do the same.”** If someone guesses the succession, enjoy the guess without lying; helpers need not protect a twist at the expense of a friendly interaction. Practical scoring questions receive truthful answers.

Repeat three motifs lightly: **a space left open, a name remembered, a welcome extended**. Reserve explicit crown, throne, sovereign and succession language for private documents and the finale. The existing allowance for stronger volunteer rumors remains available; it is not required by this treatment.

## Finale — the name the wood will keep

Release only after the host/admin has resolved the agreed ranking rules and frozen results. Use the existing default closing proposal, **November 1, 2026, 2 a.m. EST (`2026-11-01T02:00:00-05:00`, America/New_York)**, or the admin's updated deadline. “Closing” and “turning” are story terms, not a second midnight or clock-rollback trigger.

### Website sequence

1. **THE COURT IS CLOSED.** “Its witnesses have been heard.”
2. **A NAME FOR THE WINTER.** “The Hollow keeps a mortal name through the dark season. Tonight, the Antler King's keeping comes to an end.”
3. **THE OLD LAW.** “He may welcome the gathering. He may not choose who follows him. That name must be carried by the creatures, by hospitality, and by the judgment of the room.”
4. **YOUR TESTIMONY.** “Each Favor gave a creature's testimony. Each completed Summons brought a new guest into the Court. Your choices in the Looking Glass determined its costume judgment. Together, these formed the succession rite.”
5. Reveal the finalized third and second places using the agreed tie/result handling. Present the costume distinction and its agreed bonus clearly with the result breakdown; do not imply a separate uncounted election.
6. **[NAME] — SOVEREIGN OF THE HOLLOW COURT.** “Tonight, the wood learned your name. It will keep it until the next turning.” Show the portrait and earned sigils; full collection is not required.

This short sequence supplies the premise and payoff even to a guest who read no chapters. The score winner and any different physical crown recipient must remain clearly distinguished if the eventual eligibility/absence policy requires it. Never promise that a particular present guest will be crowned before those rules are settled.

### Host script — proposed 60–90-second ceremony, including handoff

> “One small piece of unfinished business before the wood lets this evening go.
>
> The Hollow keeps a mortal name through winter. For one season, it has kept mine. Tonight that season ends.
>
> Its old law says I cannot choose who follows me. So the creatures asked for your attention. The Summons asked you to make room for someone new. The Looking Glass asked the gathering what it would remember.
>
> Every one of those things was testimony. Together, you have carried out the succession of the Hollow Court.
>
> [Name], the Court has learned your name. Will you accept its crown?”

After acceptance, give the **separate winner crown**; the host keeps the Antler King headpiece. End with:

> “Be welcome, Sovereign of the Hollow Court. May your gatherings be strange, and your guests find room. The wood remembers. The party continues.”

No speech, oath, future hosting promise, kneeling or performance is required. Presence, acceptance and any alternate recipient follow the rules still awaiting review. If results are held or unavailable, hold the competitive reveal and use the existing runbook's failure procedure; this script does not fabricate a winner.

Post-finale archive line: **“Take your name back into the ordinary world. The wood has kept a little of the sound.”** The game ending does not end the party.
