## Recommended direction
The bathtub becomes a dry woodland well. A partly open forest curtain reveals a pale moon caught in roots, with a small covered vessel of chocolate coins at the accessible front corner. A guest notices the scene, takes one gift and a tiny fortune, and continues with their evening. It should take about 15 seconds to understand.

The restrained palette is charcoal, deep green, bone white and a little tarnished gold. Use one large shape and two small pools of light rather than filling the entire bathtub with props.

## Three possible scenes
| Concept | What the guest sees | What they take | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moon Well — recommended** | A pale moon rising from dark cloth and a few attached roots, behind a partly open curtain | Gold chocolate coin and a paper blessing | Low; strongest connection to existing room name |
| The Sleeping Forest God | A small sculptural root-and-skull head resting in the tub; implied body under dark fabric | Chocolate “seeds” in a separate covered bowl | Medium; eerie folk horror, needs another suitable prop or an explicit reallocation |
| The Crown the Woods Reclaimed | A lightweight twig crown nested in roots, illuminated like an abandoned relic | A folded leaf-shaped omen, optionally with chocolate | Low; stronger succession-story connection without announcing the contest |

Pick one focal point. Do not combine all three. Any lore here is original party fiction, not a claim about Samhain practice.

## Dry build plan
1. Photograph the tub, curtain rail, nearby shelves and the view from the door. Measure the curtain drop/width and accessible dry display surfaces.
2. Clean and fully dry the tub. Lay a washable dark cloth inside it, with a non-scratch protective layer under any hard props. Keep fabric entirely within the tub and off the bathroom floor.
3. Stand a lightweight 12–18-inch paper/card moon on a broad concealed base at the far end. A recycled-cardboard moon is sufficient; do not buy a large moon lamp for this version.
4. Arrange a few lightweight faux roots around the base. Attach any moss to a removable scenic tray; no loose moss, soil or foliage around drains. Keep taps accessible and do not use this display while showering.
5. Reuse the two bathroom battery candles on stable, dry surfaces. No mains lighting, candles with flames, standing water or dry ice is needed. Leave normal sink, lock and floor visibility intact.
6. Hang the chosen curtain on the existing hardware and leave it partly open to frame the scene. No heavy props hung from the curtain rod.
7. Put the covered gift container on an existing stable bath caddy or an adjacent dry shelf at the near end. Do not improvise a narrow or unstable bridge. If there is no safe reachable surface, place gifts beside the sink and let the tub supply the reveal.
8. Rehearse the view and one-handed retrieval. Guests must not step into the tub, lean over scenery or handle scenic props to participate.

## Chocolate offering
Gold coins are the first choice: they look like tiny relics and feel immediately Halloween-appropriate. Plan one per expected guest plus roughly 20% reserve: about 54 for 45 guests or 72 for 60. Counts in supplier packs can be approximate. This replaces the existing sweet-piece allowance in the food plan; it is not a second candy order.

Keep original wrappers intact. Place coins in small closed food-safe favor bags with the paper fortune outside the food wrapper, then keep them in a clean covered container separate from all scenic material. Retain ingredient/allergen packaging. Chocolate coins may contain milk and other allergens; the paper fortune is a nonfood alternative. Put a matching small reserve at the living-room food table for guests who prefer collecting food there.

Set out 15–20 at a time; hospitality helper replenishes during existing 45–60-minute checks. Guests can take the gift away before opening it. For a cheaper version, use ordinary individually wrapped chocolates in the same presentation; no specialist candy mold is needed.

## Small signs and fortunes
Display card:

> The well keeps its secrets, but shares its gold. Take one gift for the road.

Optional smaller line:

> Leave the roots undisturbed.

Print a shuffled mix of these invented blessings on small ivory slips:
- May the woods remember your kindness.
- Carry a little moonlight home.
- A lost path will find you.
- Tonight, the raven keeps your secret.
- May the last leaf wait for you.
- The roots know your true name.
- A small kindness will return threefold.
- Leave room for a little wildness.

## Relationship to the succession game
This discovery is optional and unscored. The existing scored **Well** totem remains outside the bathroom door to avoid occupying the only bathroom with a hunt. A volunteer can say, “The well sometimes gives back what the forest takes.” No volunteer waits inside, no guest needs to scan anything to receive candy, and gifts are not collected as points.

Keep bathroom lore on the existing paper blessing/sign or in volunteers’ stories. The host has rejected QR codes for interactivity. An optional future NFC lore object would need its own mounted test; it is not a new required website feature or scored bathroom checkpoint.
