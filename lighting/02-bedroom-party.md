# Bedroom • temporary club lighting
Updated September 3, 2026. Host wants a dance/club feel for the party, without a permanent bedroom conversion. Supersedes a plan that assumes existing bedroom lamps are sufficient. No rental booking or purchase.

## Recommended look
Start with two compact LED stage wash lights, independent of the living-room home-lighting system. Use one deep red/amber sidelight to reveal the Rootkeeper’s branches and one violet/blue wash across a visible wall/ceiling. Test on actual surfaces: black velvet absorbs light, so pointing every light into it wastes much of the effect. Keep steady low light at the door and clear routes.

Set fixtures at protected room edges on stable manufacturer-approved supports. No unsecured tall lighting stand in the dance area. Aim beams above/beside eyes rather than directly at dancers. Cables stay along the perimeter, not beneath loose turf or rugs. This setup uses illuminated surfaces; it does not rely on haze to make beams visible. No laser, rapid strobe or fog machine is in the default shopping list.

## Music reaction without rainbow chaos
A fixture’s microphone responds to sound levels, not the DJ track’s beat grid. More importantly, many sound programs choose their own colors. The SlimPAR Q6 ILS manufacturer guide lists a **19-color sound-active snap** program. It is therefore not evidence of a built-in red/violet-only music mode.

Start with static color/dimming and test any built-in fade or pulse before selecting it. If synchronized red/violet changes are essential, specify controllable DMX fixtures and a compatible controller/interface. That adds hardware and rehearsal; DMX/ILS/D-Fi logos alone do not mean the Mac can drive them wirelessly. A USB connector for D-Fi is not assumed to be a computer lighting interface.

[Manufacturer Q6 ILS guide](https://fr.chauvetdj.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/SlimPAR_Q6_ILS_QRG_Rev2_ML6.pdf) documents individual RGBA levels, dimmer, strobe/program speed and sound sensitivity. Program selection and color restrictions must be tested on the exact unit rented or bought. Use a strobe-off mode for the default scene.

The Mac already handles DJ and TV duties. Prefer a self-contained/manual baseline with an easy reset. Add laptop cue control only after a light-only rehearsal proves it is reliable and does not interfere with audio. No lighting code or integration has been built.

## Proposed evening arc
- Arrival: darker static red and violet; guardian visible.
- Build toward midnight: modest increase in brightness and occasional slow movement/pulse, if the chosen fixture allows the desired palette.
- Midnight–1 a.m.: strongest scene, with restrained dynamics. This does not require rapid flashing or automatic rainbow mode.
- After peak: gradually simplify and dim, retaining practical route light.
- Close: warm/white useful light for leaving and cleanup.

Timing remains aligned with the music plan and the daylight-saving rollback. The final cue schedule must use explicit timezone-aware times rather than an ambiguous repeated 1 a.m.
