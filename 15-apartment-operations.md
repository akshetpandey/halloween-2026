## Capacity
30–60 total visitors does not guarantee low simultaneous attendance: many will aim for midnight. Determine the comfortable peak from actual unobstructed space, access, ventilation, bathroom queue and building requirements. Do not publish “60 capacity” from the current brief.

Use RSVPs and rough arrival windows, one entry contact and a helper who can pause additional arrivals if the space is full. Keep visitors out of shared hallways and stairwells.

## Sound plan
The host expects Halloween to be more relaxed. Treat that as social context, not an exemption. NYC’s official residential-noise guidance still applies. We have not verified a special Halloween exception, and commercial venue decibel thresholds should not be copied onto this apartment. [NYC DEP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dep/environment/noise-code.page)

Practical sequence:
- Test actual speakers and bass from outside the apartment and, with permission, adjacent spaces.
- Keep speakers away from windows and shared structures where feasible.
- Reduce low frequencies first. Avoid adding a subwoofer.
- Curtains help the look and may reduce some room reflections; do not budget them as soundproofing.
- Foam floor padding is not a bass isolation system. Any material placed under a speaker must preserve stability.
- Set a verified maximum at the speaker/amp, then schedule only downward software changes after the peak.
- Keep a host reachable for complaints and reduce sound promptly.
- Plan airflow without assuming windows can stay wide open beside loud speakers.
- Transition the late stretch to quieter music/socializing if the apartment cannot contain club volume.

The 4 a.m. goal is a hosting preference, not a verified permissible sound level or guaranteed feasible dance finish.

## Foreseeable build issues
| Original idea | Practical concern | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Loose moss/leaves covering floors | Slips, spills, cleanup and trip edges | Contained scenic trays / attached material at edges |
| Hanging real branches overhead | Unknown overhead loads and falling pieces | Wall silhouettes or lightweight garlands on appropriate supports |
| Flash paper in a bowl | Flame near combustible decor/costumes | Private paper offering and light cue |
| Bed tied upright | Heavy unstable load | Dismantle/store flat, or keep flat and reduce dance area |
| Full black velvet coverage | Weight, cost, heat, blocked features | One backdrop and measured window treatment |
| Soft foam dance floor | Shifting seams and difficult footing | Firm existing floor; test any non-slip covering |
