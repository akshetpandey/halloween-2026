## Design recommendation
Build layered, indirect light that works every evening. For the Halloween scene, combine warm low-level light on people with deeper amber behind the trees and a small cool moon accent. Keep the living room calm; music response belongs to the bedroom.

Provisional layout, adjusted after photos:
- Two wall-facing lights in separated corners, bouncing off pale walls rather than shining into eyes.
- One low light behind the couch/TV area to soften contrast, kept subtle enough that the films remain legible.
- One shaded/table-height light near social seating so faces and drinks are visible.
- Existing practical lighting remains available for cleaning, reading, spills and setup; do not assume ambient fixtures alone replace all task illumination.

Use 2200–2700 K as an initial warm-white design target where the hardware supports it, with restrained amber color accents. These are starting scene preferences, not specifications claimed for Oasis. A little muted blue/violet near the moon can suggest the sky after sunset; it should occupy a small part of the room. One RGB sunset projector would create a focal circle but does not supply the whole layered scene.

The extensive windows will be reflective after dark. Test glare from seated height and view from the couch. Wall color, available ledges and curtain coverage determine how effective the bounce will be. Keep light sources and power supplies clear of scenic fabric; do not bury lamps beneath foliage.

## Everyday and party scenes
- **Everyday dusk:** steady low warm light at two corners and seating; enough to move around and see faces.
- **October Grove:** deeper amber behind tree props, slightly dimmer seating glow, a restrained cool moon accent. No TV color-following; films should not change the room’s lighting continuously.
- **Late night:** reduce accent intensity while retaining steady paths and drink visibility.
- **Normal/reset:** one simple action restores useful white light. Keep a physical/manual path where supported; test it rather than relying only on a phone.

The Mac is not required for ordinary daily lighting. Scheduled controls are a later integration task after exact models and supported interfaces are known. Do not assume the two Google Homes can control Oasis or that Matter carries every vendor-specific scene. Avoid automatically applying bedtime/adaptive routines during the party if they would override the chosen scene.
