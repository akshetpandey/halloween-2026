## What the guide actually means

[Celtic WebMerchant's primary guide](https://www.celticwebmerchant.co.uk/blogs/tips-tricks-outfit-accessories/make-a-headgear-from-antler) was read directly. In plain language:

1. **A wide, rigid, fitted base sits on the head.** For this unknown-weight natural pair the prior proposal is a cap-like support hidden under an ivory hood, not a thin decorative hairband. Exact base/load spread is still unselected.
2. **A pilot hole goes into the solid butt of each antler from underneath.** A corresponding hole goes through the headpiece base. Work off the body with the part securely clamped and appropriate eye/dust protection; no hand-held drilling shortcut.
3. **A screw goes upward from inside the base into the antler.** The screw head stays inside; thin padding covers it toward the head. Our proposed washer/load-spread addition is planning, not a part validated by that guide. Hardware length/diameter must match the actual antler and base; no universal size prescribed here.
4. **Hide the base and deal separately with the veil.** The full high canopy needs an independent lightweight support and accessible release, not fabric pulled across sharp antler tips. A brow-hung curtain is easier but is not the requested reference silhouette.

The guide also uses glue in the drilled holes. It gives no particular adhesive or universal cure time. Any chosen adhesive's full manufacturer cure remains additional elapsed time; do not count “sets in minutes” as a finished wearable joint. A single screw does not by itself establish anti-rotation for our unchosen uneven bases. Fit, rigid seating and resistance to twisting remain unresolved.

The inline exploded cutaway shown in the response explains only the attachment stack. It is not a finished headpiece rendering, scale drawing, tested mount or verified shopping kit. The visualize skill was used specifically to clarify the physical screw/base/padding relationship; it did not clear the sourcing/build gate.

These are judgment-based ranges, not times claimed by the guide or measured by the agent. Shopping/travel, fabric seam-unpicking, base fabrication, corrective sawing/filing and adhesive cure are **not** included. Raw sheds plus a newly invented no-drill cradle are therefore **not a reliable couple-of-hours plan**. The small drill's adequacy cannot be determined without its model, bit and workholding; no reason to buy a bigger drill yet.

**Two-hour decision rule:** only proceed as a short assembly if the exact pair's geometry/mass is known, the base is already fitted, the mechanical seats/holes are prepared and compatible, and the veil is a simple pre-sized panel with a ready support. None of those preparation conditions is currently verified. Pre-drilled antlers alone would not solve base fit, rotation or canopy construction. If the deadline/time cap is strict, do not buy raw real antlers on the promise this guide makes it plug-and-play. A ready-mounted lightweight headpiece is the lower-build-work route, but no excellent timely new one was found here; don't silently substitute earlier rejected shapes.
